## CHAPTER 1: PREAMBLE, JURISDICTION, AND GENERAL CODE OF CONDUCT

### Section 1.1: Institutional Identity and Governance
The Apex Institute of Technology (AIT) is an autonomous, premier technical institution established under the State Higher Technical Education Act. All students admitted to the four-year Bachelor of Technology (B.Tech) degree programs across all engineering disciplines are bound by the statutory regulations, disciplinary ordinances, and academic codes formulated by the AIT Academic Council and Board of Governors.

The official communication channel between the Institute and students is the institutional email address (`@apex.edu.in`) and the automated Student Information Portal (SIP). Students are required to inspect both portals at least once every 24 hours on instructional working days. Ignorance of notices published on SIP shall not be accepted as an excuse for non-compliance.

### Section 1.2: General Student Conduct and Anti-Ragging Ordinances
AIT adheres to a statutory Zero-Tolerance Policy toward ragging, harassment, cyber-bullying, and substance abuse. 

1. **Definition of Ragging**: Any disorderly conduct, whether by words spoken or written, or by an act that causes annoyance, psychological discomfort, physical abuse, or monetary extortion to any student.
2. **Statutory Penalties**: Any student established to be guilty of ragging by the Standing Proctorial Committee shall face immediate summary suspension for a minimum of two semesters or permanent expulsion with registration of a First Information Report (FIR) with jurisdictional police authorities.
3. **Campus Civility & Identification**: All registered students must wear their biometric Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) Smart Cards visibly around the neck at all times inside academic blocks, laboratories, workshops, central library, and hostel precincts. Security personnel are authorized to deny entry or levy an administrative fine of ₹200 for failure to display RFID badges upon spot inspection.
4. **Prohibited Materials**: Possession, distribution, or consumption of alcohol, tobacco products, e-cigarettes, vaping devices, or contraband narcotics within the 120-acre institutional boundary will attract immediate expulsion from campus hostels, a non-negotiable penalty of ₹25,000, and mandatory referral to the District Narcotics Control Bureau.

## CHAPTER 2: ACADEMIC CALENDAR, COURSE REGISTRATION, AND CREDIT SCHEME

### Section 2.1: Credit Architecture and Degree Requirements
The four-year B.Tech degree curriculum is structured across eight regular academic semesters (Autumn Semester: August to December; Spring Semester: January to May). Each academic year consists of a minimum of 90 instructional teaching days per semester, excluding end-semester examination periods and inter-semester breaks.

The unit of academic measurement is the Academic Credit ($C$). Credits are distributed based on weekly instructional contact hours:
- **Lecture (L)**: 1 hour per week = 1.0 Credit
- **Tutorial (T)**: 1 hour per week = 1.0 Credit
- **Laboratory / Practical (P)**: 2 consecutive hours per week = 1.0 Credit
- **Industrial Internship / Minor Project**: 4 weeks supervised field engagement = 2.0 Credits

To qualify for the award of the B.Tech degree, a student must accumulate a minimum of 160.0 qualifying credits across the 8 semesters, maintaining a Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) of not less than 5.00 on a 10-point scale, with no active uncleared backlogs ("F" or "I" grades) at the conclusion of the eighth semester.

| Category of Courses | Abbreviation | Minimum Required Credits |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Basic Science Courses (Physics, Chemistry, Calculus, Linear Algebra) | BSC | 24.0 |
| Engineering Science Courses (Basic Electronics, Mechanics, Python Computing) | ESC | 22.0 |
| Humanities, Social Sciences, and Management Courses (Communication, IPR, Ethics) | HSM | 12.0 |
| Professional Core Courses (Departmental Theory & Laboratories) | PCC | 58.0 |
| Professional Elective Courses (Specialized Technical Tracks) | PEC | 18.0 |
| Open Elective Courses (Inter-disciplinary across other engineering departments) | OEC | 12.0 |
| Project Work, Seminar, and Comprehensive Technical Viva | PWC | 14.0 |
| Mandatory Non-Credit Audit Courses (Environmental Science, Constitution of India) | MNC | 0.0 (Satisfactory grade required) |
| **Total Minimum Degree Requirement** | **TOTAL** | **160.0 Credits** |

### Section 2.2: Course Registration Guidelines and Credit Limits
1. **Pre-Registration Window**: Every student must complete online course registration through the SIP portal within the scheduled 7-calendar-day pre-registration window announced in the Institute Academic Calendar.
2. **Normal Credit Load**: The standard allowable semester credit load is between 18.0 and 24.0 credits per regular semester.
3. **Credit Overload**: A student who has obtained a CGPA of $\ge 8.50$ without any backlog in the preceding two semesters may petition the Dean of Academic Affairs to register for one additional course up to a ceiling of 28.0 credits.
4. **Credit Underload**: A student placed on Academic Probation (CGPA $< 5.00$) may be restricted by the Department Academic Committee (DAC) to a reduced credit load of between 12.0 and 16.0 credits to facilitate remedial progression.
5. **Late Registration Fine**: Course registration completed after the designated deadline, but within the subsequent 5 working days, shall incur a mandatory late registration fee of ₹1,000. Registration submitted between the 6th and 10th working day shall incur a late fee of ₹2,500. Under no circumstances is registration permitted after the 10th working day from the semester commencement date.
6. **Course Add/Drop Period**: Students may drop or add an elective course within 10 calendar days of the start of classes, provided the final registered credits remain within the prescribed 18.0 to 24.0 credit boundaries. Core courses cannot be dropped.

## CHAPTER 3: ATTENDANCE RULES, LEAVE REGULATIONS, AND CONDONATION

### Section 3.1: Minimum Attendance Mandate
At AIT, regular attendance in all scheduled instructional classes (lectures, tutorials, and laboratory practical sessions) is a mandatory condition precedent for appearing in the End-Semester Examination (ESE).

1. **Mandatory Minimum Attendance Floor**: A student must maintain an aggregate physical attendance of at least **75.0%** in each individual registered course.
2. **Attendance Calculation Formula**:
   $$\text{Attendance Percentage } (\%) = \left( \frac{\text{Total Contact Hours Attended by Student}}{\text{Total Scheduled Contact Hours Conducted by Instructor}} \right) \times 100$$
3. Attendance is captured digitally at the start of each lecture using RFID door readers and verified by the course instructor on the SIP portal. Any discrepancy must be reported in writing to the Head of Department (HoD) within 3 working days of occurrence.

### Section 3.2: Medical and Co-Curricular Condonation Slabs
The Institute recognizes that unforeseen health emergencies or authorized official representations may preclude physical presence. Condonation of attendance shortage is governed strictly by the following statutory brackets:

| Attendance Percentage Bracket | Eligibility Category | Required Supporting Documentation | Applicable Condonation Fee | Consequence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **75.0% and above** | Standard Eligible | None | Nil | Permitted to sit for End-Semester Examination |
| **65.0% to 74.9%** | Medical Ground | Form M-2 signed by AIT Chief Medical Officer within 7 days of discharge | ₹1,500 per course (Max ₹5,000 per semester) | Eligible for condonation upon approval of Dean (Academic) |
| **65.0% to 74.9%** | Authorized Sports / Tech Rep | Form S-1 signed by Physical Director / Dean of Student Affairs | Nil | Permitted upon recommendation of faculty advisor |
| **60.0% to 64.9%** | Extraordinary Hospitalization / National Representation | Hospital Admission Proof (>14 continuous days) or National / International Competition certificate | ₹3,000 per course (Subject to Director's discretionary ratification) | Maximum legal exception boundary |
| **Below 60.0%** | Severe Shortage | Not applicable | No condonation permissible | **Mandatory "W" (Withdrawn) Grade**. Must re-register the course when offered next. |

### Section 3.3: Strict Condonation Exceptions and Disclaimers
1. Under **no circumstances** may any authority, including the Director or Academic Council, condone an attendance falling below 60.0%, regardless of the medical or personal justification presented.
2. Submitting forged or backdated medical certificates to claim condonation will result in an immediate referral to the Institute Discipline Committee, automatic award of an "F" grade in all registered courses for that semester, and a suspension of two semesters.
3. Maternity Leave for eligible married students is admissible up to 45 continuous calendar days upon formal medical documentation, with credit pause provisions.

## CHAPTER 4: ASSESSMENT FRAMEWORK, EXAMINATION SCHEME, AND GRADING

### Section 4.1: Continuous Internal Evaluation (CIE) and Semester End Examination (SEE)
Each registered course is assessed through an integrated dual-mode evaluation framework:
- **Continuous Internal Evaluation (CIE)**: 50% Weightage (50 Marks)
- **Semester End Examination (SEE)**: 50% Weightage (50 Marks)

The distribution of the 50 CIE marks is structured as follows:
- Mid-Semester Examination (MSE) conducted at week 8: 25 Marks (Duration: 90 Minutes)
- Continuous Class Quizzes (best 2 out of 3 quizzes): 10 Marks
- Homework Assignments / Programming Lab Benchmarks: 10 Marks
- Class Participation & Tutorial Engagement: 5 Marks

For Laboratory Courses, the 50 Continuous Internal Evaluation marks are based on weekly lab preparation and viva (20 Marks), experimental execution and observation log (20 Marks), and mini-project / code portfolio (10 Marks).

### Section 4.2: 10-Point Absolute Letter Grading System
The final marks obtained in a course ($Marks = \text{CIE} + \text{SEE}$, out of 100) are converted into letter grades using an absolute scale, unless the Academic Council mandates a normalized relative curve for large cohort courses ($> 300$ students).

| Letter Grade | Performance Descriptor | Grade Points ($G$) | Percentage Marks Range | Remarks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **O** | Outstanding | 10.0 | $\ge 90$ | Top-tier academic mastery |
| **A+** | Excellent | 9.0 | $80 - 89$ | High analytical capability |
| **A** | Very Good | 8.0 | $70 - 79$ | Thorough conceptual understanding |
| **B+** | Good | 7.0 | $60 - 69$ | Above average proficiency |
| **B** | Above Average | 6.0 | $50 - 59$ | Basic competent proficiency |
| **C** | Average / Pass Floor | 5.0 | $40 - 49$ | Minimum passing grade for individual course |
| **F** | Fail | 0.0 | $< 40$ | Backlog; must appear in Supplementary Exam |
| **W** | Withdrawn (Shortage) | 0.0 | Shortage $< 60\%$ | Must repeat whole course with attendance |
| **I** | Incomplete | 0.0 | Authorized absence | MSE/SEE missed due to verified medical cause |

### Section 4.3: Computation of SGPA and CGPA
The Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA) and Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) evaluate the student's academic standing:

$$\text{SGPA} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} (C_i \times G_i)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} C_i}$$
where $C_i$ denotes the registered credits of course $i$, and $G_i$ represents the numeric grade points obtained in course $i$.

The Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) is computed across all accumulated semesters:
$$\text{CGPA} = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{m} (C_j \times G_j)}{\sum_{j=1}^{m} C_j}$$
where $m$ denotes the total number of all credit-bearing courses completed since matriculation.

**Degree Classification Standards**:
- **First Class with Distinction**: CGPA $\ge 8.50$, completed within exactly 8 consecutive semesters without any historical backlog grade ("F" or "W").
- **First Class**: CGPA $\ge 6.75$ and $< 8.50$.
- **Second Class**: CGPA $\ge 5.00$ and $< 6.75$.
- **Academic Ineligibility**: If a student's CGPA drops below 4.50 at the end of the second academic year (4th semester), the student is issued a terminal show-cause notice and placed on a 1-year academic cool-off period.

### Section 4.4: Supplementary Examinations and Re-evaluation Protocol
1. **Supplementary Examination Eligibility**: Supplementary exams are conducted once per academic year during the summer break (June). A student with an "F" grade in an exam where attendance was $\ge 60\%$ is eligible. The maximum attainable grade in a supplementary exam is capped at **"A" (8.0)**.
2. **Re-Evaluation Request Window**: A student dissatisfied with their SEE answer script evaluation may apply for photographic script inspection and re-evaluation via the SIP portal within **10 calendar days** from the date of grade notification.
3. **Re-evaluation Fee**: A non-refundable fee of **₹1,000 per theory course** must be deposited online.
4. **Mark Alteration Rule**: The re-evaluated grade replaces the original grade **if and only if** the difference between the original and revised mark is **$\ge 5.0$ marks**. If the difference is $< 5.0$ marks, the original grade remains unchanged to prevent trivial grade inflation.

**Late Entry Penalty System**:
Every student is allotted a maximum quota of **3 Grace Late Slips per academic semester** for entry up to 60 minutes after curfew.
- **1st Violation past grace quota**: Written warning and ₹500 fine.

- **3rd Violation past grace quota**: Immediate suspension of hostel out-pass privileges for 30 days and referral to the Warden Council.
- **Unauthorized Absence (Night Out without Out-Pass)**: Attracts an automatic penalty of **₹5,000**, immediate summoning of parents to campus, and potential eviction from the hostel.

Electrical heating coils, induction cooktops, immersion rods, and electric heaters are strictly contraband. Discovery of prohibited electrical appliances during routine warden inspections incurs a spot confiscation and a non-negotiable fine of **₹3,000 per appliance**.

1. Undergraduate access to the HPC cluster is granted only to students enrolled in 7th/8th semester capstone projects or sponsored faculty research upon written endorsement of the Project Supervisor.

## CHAPTER 9: ACADEMIC INTEGRITY, PLAGIARISM PROTOCOLS, AND DISCIPLINARY ACTIONS

### Section 9.1: Plagiarism Limits in Capstone Projects and Theses
All capstone project reports, technical seminar papers, and research manuscripts authored by students must be vetted through the institutional plagiarism detection system (Turnitin) prior to final submission.

The Institute adheres to UGC-mandated similarity index thresholds (excluding bibliography, quotes, and mathematical formulas):
- **Level 0 (Similarity $\le 10\%$)**: Fully acceptable. Student is cleared for formal viva voce defense.
- **Level 1 (Similarity $> 10\%$ to $\le 40\%$)**: Deficient report. Student is granted a grace period of **14 calendar days** to revise the manuscript and resubmit without penalty.
- **Level 2 (Similarity $> 40\%$ to $\le 60\%$)**: Severe breach. Student is disqualified from defending the capstone project in the current semester and must repeat the project across the following semester with an entirely new problem statement.
- **Level 3 (Similarity $> 60\%$)**: Willful academic fraud. Student is awarded an **"F" grade** in the capstone course, suspended for one full academic year, and barred from campus placement drives.

### Section 9.2: Examination Malpractice and Unfair Means Code (UMC)
Possession of unauthorized materials inside the examination hall (such as handwritten chits, smart watches, programmable calculators with stored memory, or smartphones) constitutes an immediate act of Unfair Means (UMC):

- **UMC Category A (Possession without consultation)**: Student found in possession of notes or electronic devices, but no evidence of copying. Consequence: Cancellation of the examination in that particular course; awarded an "F" grade.
- **UMC Category B (Active copying or communication)**: Student observed copying from notes, digital screens, or communicating answers to peers. Consequence: Cancellation of all registered examinations in that entire semester; awarded "F" grades in all courses; student debarred from registering for the subsequent summer supplementary term.
- **UMC Category C (Impersonation / Forgery of answer script)**: Student found impersonating another candidate or attempting to replace an official answer script booklet. Consequence: Permanent expulsion from the Institute and handover to local law enforcement.
